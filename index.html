Carvable

Ready for release Blender 4.5 LTS – 5.2 LTS GPL-3.0-or-later

Turns an image or height map into an exact bas-relief for CNC carving and 3D printing: sizes in millimetres, draft angles for tapered bits, a check of what your router bit can really carve, a closed STL and a 16-bit height map for your CAM program.

  • Exact millimetres. Plate width, height, relief depth, stock thickness and base are entered in mm; the STL has exactly the size you asked for.
  • Draft angle for tapered bits. No wall is steeper than the angle you set (default 10° from vertical). Material is added at the foot of steep walls; tops are never lowered.
  • Tool check. Pick a tapered ball nose, ball nose, flat end mill or V-bit and its size. The preview colours every spot the bit cannot reach and reports the uncut thickness. Export as Carved writes the surface the bit will actually leave.
  • Depth compression with detail kept. Linear by default; a gradient-domain mode (after Weyrich et al., SIGGRAPH 2007) squeezes deep scenes while fine texture survives.
  • 16-bit everywhere. 16-bit PNG and TIFF and 32-bit EXR are read without colour management, so every level of a height map is kept. 8-bit stepping is smoothed away.
  • Outputs for CAM. A closed (watertight) STL in millimetres and a 16-bit PNG height map with a dimensions file for Vectric, Carveco, PixelCNC and others.

Framekeeper

Coming after Carvable Blender 4.5 LTS – 5.2 LTS 9:16 · 4:5 · 3:4 · 1:1 · 16:9 GPL-3.0-or-later

Camera moves that frame your product themselves: a 2 cm ring or a 1.6 m workbench, in every format from vertical to 16:9. Every frame is computed, and every frame is checked.

  • Exact, not guessed. For every frame the camera distance is solved from the product’s convex hull and the edges of the safe zone. In our tests the camera matches Blender’s own projection to within 0.000001 of the frame.
  • Any size, one shot. Shots are made of composition, not camera positions. Every shot is tested on a ring, a bottle and a car in every format: 210 combinations, the product inside the safe zone in every frame.
  • Made for vertical video. The 9:16 safe zone keeps the product clear of the captions and buttons that Meta’s Reels ad guide reserves for the app’s interface.
  • Check Shots. One click lists the frames where the product leaves the safe zone, something hides it, the camera is inside an object, or motion blur pushes it out.
  • Capture from Camera. Any existing camera move becomes a shot: replayed exactly on the same product, re-framed on another product or in another format.
  • Your scene stays yours. Framekeeper never changes your objects; Remove Cameras deletes only what it made.

Stairwright

Coming after Framekeeper Blender 4.5 LTS – 5.2 LTS Straight · L · U · spiral GPL-3.0-or-later

Stairs and railings you can actually build, not just render. Give it the floor-to-floor height: it works out the steps from the rules you choose, runs one railing along the flight, round the landing and round the opening above, and shows at once whether everything is within the limits.

  • From the floor height. Straight, L, U or spiral, in steel side plates, closed wooden stringers or cut stringers. Every rise and every going equal, in one click.
  • One railing, anywhere. Up the flight, round the landing and round the opening in the floor above: balusters, bars, glass or a solid wall. Railing from Selection puts the same railing on any edge of your scene.
  • Measured, not assumed. The check measures the finished mesh, not the settings: rises and goings equal to 0.001 mm, headroom with rays every 10 mm, the largest gap in every railing against the code’s sphere.
  • Rules with sources. Presets for the USA (IRC 2024, IBC 2024), the UK (Approved Document K), Canada (NBC 2020), Australia (NCC housing) and Poland (WT 2002), plus a comfort rule. It measures against them; it does not certify compliance.
  • Fits your space. Select a floor with a stairwell and get the stairs that fit it, with the presets each one passes.
  • For makers. A parts list with cut angles and 1:1 outlines of stringers, treads and risers as DXF and SVG, in millimetres or inches.

How our add-ons are made

The code is written with an AI coding assistant (Claude by Anthropic), working to specifications written and approved by Larklock. Larklock chooses what to build and for whom, sets the goals and limits, reviews the results and makes the product decisions: scope, price, name, which camera shots go into the library and which stair styles ship. Every feature is covered by automated tests on Blender 4.5, 5.0, 5.1 and 5.2. The add-ons themselves contain no AI and work offline. The product images and demo films are rendered in Blender from each add-on’s own output, and every camera move in the Framekeeper media was computed by Framekeeper. Only the walnut and oak wood colours in the Carvable media and the Framekeeper images, and the out-of-focus workshop behind the Carvable bench, come from images generated with Google’s image model.
